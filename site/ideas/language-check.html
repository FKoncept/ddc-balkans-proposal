Language check

Every role at DDC is defined by a language and a level. This lets candidates show their level in a few minutes, lets a colleague who speaks the language judge it without a phone call, and puts the result on the candidate's record.

In short

I can't judge Norwegian or Czech myself, so for every candidate I have to find a colleague and a time that suits everyone.

What changes
The candidate records two or three short spoken answers and writes one short reply, on their phone, in about ten minutes. A colleague who speaks the language listens when it suits them and picks a level. The result appears on the candidate's record.

First, a question for you

You very likely test languages already, and some of your clients may require a specific test. We could not find how it works today, so we would like to hear it before building anything.

If you already have a test that works, we build around it: send the link from the candidate list, and put the result on the record. This page shows what we would build if you want something new, or want to replace a process that takes too much of your time.

Why it matters for this job

  • Every ad sets a levelThe German Customer Support Agent ad asks for "minimum B2". The level is the first thing that decides whether someone can move forward.
  • Different roles, different skillsDDC's data entry ad on poslovi.ba says reading and writing matter more than pronunciation. Support roles are the opposite. One test does not fit both.
  • Eleven languagesNobody can personally judge Norwegian, Czech, Hungarian and Dutch. Today that likely means finding a colleague who speaks it and arranging a call around their shift.
  • Candidates waitEvery day between "applied" and "we know their level" is a day another employer can call them first.

What the candidate sees

A link on their phone or laptop, sent automatically or by you. Two or three short spoken answers recorded in the browser, and one short written reply. About ten minutes, no app to install.

Question sets per language and role

Each role type gets its own set. For example:

  • Customer support: three spoken answers to typical customer situations, one short email reply.
  • Data entry: one short spoken introduction, two written tasks, such as correcting and summarising a short text.
  • B2B sales: a short spoken pitch and a follow-up email.

We draft the questions in English with you. Your own speakers of each language adapt them, because a good question in Norwegian needs a Norwegian speaker. We do not write target-language tests alone.

What your colleague sees

A link to one review. They listen, read, pick a level and add a note if they want. The reviewer sees a candidate code, not a name or photo, so the rating is about the language only.

More detail, if you want it

How we would run it
  1. Hear how you test today, and which roles need which skills.
  2. Draft the question sets in English with you; your speakers adapt them per language.
  3. Build and try it with a few colleagues playing candidates, on phones and laptops.
  4. Choose reviewers for each language, and agree how quickly a review should be done.
  5. Go live for one or two languages first, then add the rest.
What's included, and what isn't

Included

  • Candidate page with recording and written tasks, on phone and laptop
  • Reviewer page with the level scale and notes
  • Question sets for the role types you choose, drafted with you
  • Results on the record (with change 1) or by email
  • Recordings deleted automatically after the decision, on the schedule you set

Not included, or needs a decision

  • Automatic scoring by software: a person always decides the level
  • An official certificate: this is an internal check
  • Writing questions in each language without your speakers
  • Replacing a test an end client requires
