In short

I copy every application into Excel, and the file keeps going out of date. Sometimes the same person is in there twice, with two different statuses.

What changes
Applications appear in one shared list on their own. When someone applies again, it is added to their existing record. People who work here, or asked not to be contacted, never appear in your call list.

Why the Excel keeps going wrong

It is not a discipline problem. A spreadsheet has no idea that row 14 and row 212 are the same person, it cannot notice that someone was contacted a week ago and never answered, and it does not stop you from typing "to contact" next to someone who started last month.

Every copy from WordPress into Excel is also a chance for a typo, a missed application or a second row for the same person. The fix is to take the copying out completely, and let the list check itself.

What you get

  • Applications arrive on their ownA small add-on to your WordPress site sends each new application, CV included, to the list as soon as it is submitted. We checked the job plugin's code: it announces every new application, which is exactly what the add-on listens for. Candidates see no change.
  • One record per personA new application with an email or phone number already in the list is attached to that person's record, with a clear note that they applied again.
  • Your own statusesWe use the statuses you use today (for example New, Contacted, Interview, Offer, Working, Left, Not suitable, Do not contact), grouped by colour.
  • Full historyEvery change shows who made it and when, with an optional note. No more guessing why a row says what it says.
  • GuardrailsWorking and do-not-contact people never appear in call lists. Trying to contact them shows a warning with the reason.
  • Search and filtersBy name, email, phone, language, city, role and status. Filters by language level and city work fully once change 2 adds those fields.
  • Your Excel importedWe bring over the existing sheet, and show you likely duplicates side by side to confirm before anything is merged.
  • Export any timeDownload the whole list to Excel whenever you want. Your data is never locked in.
  • Named accessEach person who uses the list has their own login. You decide who that is, and can remove access at any time.
  • Chat buttonsOne click opens WhatsApp or Viber with the candidate's number filled in, and a call button on phones.

More detail, if you want it

Applications from other places

This change covers applications sent through your careers website. We noticed DDC also advertises on MojPosao.ba, LinkedIn and poslovi.ba, and some older ads ask for CVs by email.

If you want those in the same list too, we can look into what each channel allows and come back with a plan. We are not promising it yet, because we have not checked what MojPosao and LinkedIn let us export. Until then, anything that arrives elsewhere can be added by hand, and the duplicate check still applies.

Doesn't the job plugin already do some of this?

Partly, and it is worth knowing before you decide. WP Job Openings (now called HireZoot) keeps every application inside WordPress. Its paid Pro pack adds shortlisting, rejecting, rating and exporting applications.

What it is built around is the application, not the person. It does not merge several applications from the same person, it does not hold the history you have in Excel today, and it has no idea who currently works here or asked not to be contacted. Those three gaps are exactly where your mix-ups come from, so they are what this change adds. If you already have Pro, we will say plainly which parts you can use as they are.

How we would run it
  1. One hour with your Excel. You show us how you really use it. Column names only, no personal data needs to leave your computer.
  2. Agree the statuses and rules. Which statuses exist, which ones hide a person from call lists, what counts as a duplicate.
  3. Build the list and the WordPress connection. You get a test version with sample data to click through and comment on.
  4. Import the Excel. Together we review the likely duplicates.
  5. Two weeks side by side. New applications land in the list; you keep Excel open as a safety net.
  6. Retire the Excel. Kept as a read-only archive, not deleted.
